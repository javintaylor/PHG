Where PHG fits in the care continuum.

PHG gives healthcare teams a practical pathway when an adult needs an in-home intervention or aide support without an insurance-based home health episode.

Extend a defined care need into the home without transferring primary or longitudinal medical management.

Acceptance remains subject to RN review, clinical stability, required orders, staffing, location, and home-setting appropriateness.
  • Extend care into the homeObtain assessment findings or complete ordered services in the patient's living environment without sending practice staff into the field.
  • Serve patients outside home health eligibilityPHG may fit when a patient is not homebound, needs one discrete service, or does not require a Medicare-certified plan of care.
  • Receive objective RN findingsVital signs, symptoms, medication observations, wound or device findings, interventions, and patient response are documented for the episode.
  • Protect practice capacityFor an accepted referral, PHG contacts the patient, completes intake, verifies required documentation, coordinates scheduling, and addresses visit logistics.
  • Add structured escalationConcerning findings are communicated to the appropriate provider or escalated when the patient exceeds PHG's home-care parameters.
  • Keep billing separateThe patient or responsible party elects and pays PHG directly. PHG does not submit an insurance claim through the referring practice.

A substantial population with limited flexible options.

Cecil County and Harford County include a growing adult population. Many residents are older, live with disabilities, or face mobility and transportation barriers, yet may not fit the eligibility structure of Medicare-certified Home Health.

PHG is designed for appropriate patients within this gap: clinically stable adults who need a discrete in-home service rather than emergency care or longitudinal medical management.

Different pathways for different circumstances.

PHG does not replace a Medicare-certified Home Health Agency. It provides another route when a clinically stable patient needs a discrete service or RN-supervised aide plan.

Medicare Home Health generally requires

Current CMS eligibility and certification structure

  • The patient is confined to the home.
  • The patient is under the care of a physician or allowed practitioner.
  • Services follow an established and periodically reviewed plan of care.
  • The patient needs intermittent skilled nursing, physical therapy, or speech-language pathology.
  • Required certification and documentation standards are met.
Review CMS Home Health criteria

PHG may be appropriate when

A discrete private-pay service is needed

  • The patient is not homebound but has difficulty accessing a service site.
  • The patient needs one ordered procedure or a limited episodic series.
  • A certified Home Health episode has ended and a new discrete need has developed.
  • The service is outside another agency's licensed level or operational capacity.
  • The patient or responsible party elects private-pay service.

Common referral scenarios, grouped by clinical need.

These examples describe episodic requests PHG may accept. Every referral is reviewed for stability, orders, staffing, location, and appropriateness for the home setting.

Assessment and transition supportPost-discharge assessment and interim episodic support

Post-discharge assessment

A patient needs focused RN assessment, medication reconciliation, education, and red-flag screening after an emergency department or hospital visit.

Interim episodic support

A patient is waiting for another appropriate service to begin and has a defined need that can be safely addressed through PHG.

Medication, wound, and post-operative careOrdered administration, dressing changes, and focused recovery support

Ordered medication administration

A clinically stable patient needs an IV, IM, SQ, inhaled, or other provider-ordered medication administered at home.

Wound or post-operative care

A patient needs ordered dressing changes, incision assessment, wound observation, or suture or staple removal.

Device and enteral supportOstomy, catheter, tracheostomy, and feeding-tube needs

Ostomy or urinary catheter service

A patient needs an ordered ostomy intervention, catheter insertion or exchange, or focused device-related assessment.

Tracheostomy or enteral-tube support

A stable patient needs an ordered episodic assessment, intervention, troubleshooting, or caregiver reinforcement.

Mobile diagnostic acquisitionSpecimen collection and 12-lead EKG acquisition

Mobile specimen collection

A provider has ordered laboratory testing and the patient has difficulty reaching the designated collection site.

12-lead EKG acquisition

A clinician needs a tracing acquired at home for interpretation and medical decision-making by the authorized provider.

Aide and organization coordinationRN-planned assistance and cross-organization clinical coordination

Aide support outside skilled Home Health

A patient needs personal care and mobility assistance, safety support, check-ins, or appointment accompaniment under an RN plan.

Cross-organization clinical coordination

Another home-care organization requests coordination because the client needs an ordered RN intervention that organization cannot provide.

Know what requires an order before referring.

PHG verifies that orders are complete, current, within scope, and appropriate for the proposed setting. Some medication pathways may require additional clinical authorization.

Provider order required

  • Medication administrationMedication, dose, route, timing, indication, and applicable monitoring parameters.
  • Wound care and dressing changesLocation, treatment instructions, products, frequency, precautions, and escalation parameters.
  • Ostomy care and appliance supportOrdered intervention, appliance or supply requirements, frequency, and condition-specific precautions.
  • Urinary catheter servicesCatheter type and size, insertion or exchange instructions, specimen needs, and clinical parameters.
  • Tracheostomy supportDefined episodic intervention, equipment or supply needs, and airway-specific precautions.
  • Enteral feeding-tube supportTube type, requested intervention, feeding or flushing parameters, supplies, and precautions.
  • Mobile phlebotomyLaboratory tests, diagnosis or indication when required, designated laboratory, and handling instructions.

Acquisition request

PHG needs the 12-lead EKG acquisition request and designated destination for the tracing. Order and documentation requirements are confirmed during RN review. PHG does not interpret the EKG.

Case review

PHG may review other discrete RN procedures based on order clarity, nursing scope, competency, supplies, and safety in the proposed setting.

What returns to the referring team.

With appropriate patient authorization, PHG can return episode-specific information that supports continuity without assuming the provider's ongoing treatment responsibilities.

  1. Referral intake reviewConfirmation of the requested service, required documentation, patient contact, location, and basic eligibility.
  2. Focused RN assessmentRelevant vital signs, symptoms, medication observations, nursing-scope findings, and home-environment observations.
  3. Intervention recordProcedure or medication performed, supplies used, administration details, and ordered treatment completed.
  4. Patient responsePost-intervention reassessment, tolerance, education, and condition at the conclusion of the visit.
  5. Escalation and communicationDocumentation of provider notification, urgent referral, EMS activation, or other escalation when applicable.
  6. Episode-specific summaryA clinical summary may be transmitted to the authorized referring provider according to patient authorization and PHG policy.

Practice value, stated honestly.

PHG can extend service reach and reduce episode-level coordination for an accepted referral. It does not create a referral payment or guarantee a financial result.

Extend the practice without sending the practice into the field.

The referring clinician retains medical decision-making. PHG manages the accepted home-care episode within its scope and returns authorized information that helps close the loop.

Protect clinician and staff capacityPHG handles patient outreach, intake, private-pay communication, visit logistics, and scheduling for an accepted episode.
Support access outside the officeAppropriate adults can receive a focused assessment, ordered intervention, specimen collection, EKG acquisition, or RN-supervised aide plan where they live.
Return clinically useful informationWith authorization, the referring team may receive relevant findings, intervention details, patient response, education, and documented escalation.
Support broader value-based strategiesTimely in-home assessment and appropriate escalation may complement access, care-transition, utilization, and patient-experience work already led by the practice.

A defined route from referral to visit summary.

Referrals may come from healthcare professionals, care organizations, patients, or responsible parties. Orders must be obtained or verified before order-dependent services are performed.

  1. 1

    Submit referral

    Send patient contact information, requested service, relevant history, and available orders or discharge documentation.

  2. 2

    RN review

    PHG screens the request for stability, service scope, authorization requirements, supplies, service location, and staffing.

  3. 3

    Coordinate visit

    PHG contacts the patient, confirms private-pay terms, obtains required consents, and schedules the accepted service.

  4. 4

    Complete and communicate

    The nurse documents the episode, communicates urgent findings, and returns an authorized visit summary when applicable.
