Why PHG exists.

Some adults need timely, non-emergency care at home without entering a long-term home health episode. PHG was designed for that space.

The right service should begin with the person, the clinical need, and the home where care will occur.

A discrete clinical needA nursing visit, specimen collection, treatment support, or focused observation that calls for professional care at home.
Support after a covered episodeAdditional private-pay help when insurance-covered services have ended or do not fit the requested frequency.
Personal care with oversightConsistent aide support built from an RN assessment and an individualized plan of care.
Privacy and timeCare arranged around the home and the client's schedule, with scope and communication established before the visit.

The PHG Care Standard.

Our protocols translate clinical governance into a practical five-part discipline. The exact pathway depends on the PHG service and individual need.

Assess

An RN reviews needs, history, risks, home factors, and service fit before intervention or aide services begin.

Authorize

Orders, consent, and provider authorization are confirmed when the requested service requires them.

Deliver

Nurses and aides work within their roles, competencies, the plan of care, and visit-specific clinical direction.

Document

Care, observations, communication, and follow-up needs are recorded through approved, privacy-conscious systems.

Review

Clinical leadership supports quality assurance, policy review, competency, and escalation when circumstances change.

Care has a clinical backbone.

PHG combines direct care with defined supervision, medical oversight, written operating standards, and visit-level accountability.

People remain responsible for the standard.

Protocols support consistency. They do not replace professional judgment, patient-specific orders, or the duty to escalate when a need exceeds scope.

Registered nursesAssess, plan, implement, document, evaluate, coordinate, and supervise within authorized nursing practice.
Certified nursing assistantsProvide personal care and supportive services under RN direction, with observations and changes communicated promptly.
Medical Director and AdministratorSupport clinical policy, governance, quality review, and the operating boundaries that protect clients and staff.
Training and competencyRole-specific qualifications, onboarding, continuing education, and competency verification support safe delivery.
Controlled proceduresStandard operating procedures and core treatment frameworks are reviewed, versioned, and updated as practice or regulation changes.

What families can expect.

Premium care is not excess. It is thoughtful preparation, clear communication, disciplined follow-through, and respect for the home.

Clinical clarity

We establish the requested service, applicable orders, responsibilities, limitations, and follow-up plan before care whenever possible.

Discretion at home

Caregivers enter a private residence as clinicians and guests, protecting dignity, preference, and confidential information.

Connected communication

With consent, relevant updates can be shared through approved channels with the client, authorized representatives, and treating providers.

Honest boundaries

If a need is urgent, outside scope, or better served elsewhere, PHG directs the client toward the appropriate level of care.

Clear boundaries protect care.

Understanding what PHG does, and what it does not do, helps families choose the right level of support.

Clinical care delivery

Services are based on clinical review, the requested scope, staff competency, applicable orders, and a visit-specific or ongoing plan as appropriate. Core treatment frameworks support consistency while preserving professional judgment and patient-specific direction.

Private-pay services

PHG operates on a private-pay basis with published starting rates. Services are not represented as covered by Medicare, Medicaid, or any insurer. Itemized documentation may be available for clients to submit independently when applicable.

Non-emergency care

PHG does not provide emergency response. Call 911 for severe symptoms, immediate danger, or a life-threatening concern. Scheduling a PHG visit does not replace emergency or urgent evaluation.

Provider oversight and orders

Medication administration, injections, wound treatment, and other order-dependent services require appropriate provider authorization. PHG may coordinate with the treating provider when consent and secure communication are in place.

Aide scope and supervision

Aide services begin after RN assessment and plan development. Aides provide assigned personal care and support within their role and report observations or changes to the supervising nurse.

Scope limitations

PHG is not a primary care practice, emergency service, hospice agency, or Medicare-certified home health agency. PHG does not promise longitudinal disease management or elective wellness services outside its defined care model.

Privacy and secure communication

Protected health information is handled through approved systems and channels. Care coordination occurs with client authorization and only to the extent needed for the service and safe follow-up.

Consent and participation

Clients or authorized representatives participate in care decisions, provide required information and consent, and receive relevant instructions. PHG may decline or stop a service that cannot be provided safely or within scope.

Diagnostics and interpretation

PHG clinicians may collect specimens, record observations, and transmit information as authorized. They do not independently diagnose, prescribe, or provide final interpretation of laboratory or diagnostic results.

Website information

This page describes PHG's general care model and is not medical advice, a guarantee of service, or a substitute for an individual assessment. Contact PHG to determine whether a requested service is appropriate and available.
