What this service includes

A guided, personal conversation—at your own table

An advance directive works best when it is completed thoughtfully, before a crisis. PHG’s role is to make the process understandable and finished—not to tell you what to choose.

  • Plain-language education about what Maryland advance directives are: naming a health care agent and stating treatment preferences
  • Facilitated discussion of your wishes, at your pace, with family present if you want them there
  • Help locating and walking through Maryland’s free advance directive forms, section by section
  • Education about what to do with the completed document—who should receive copies, including your health care agent and your providers
  • Encouragement to revisit the document when circumstances change

Maryland’s advance directive forms are published free of charge by the Maryland Office of the Attorney General. You do not need to purchase a form or a kit to have a valid advance directive in Maryland.

How a visit works

From questions to a completed document

  1. Schedule the Visit

    Request a facilitation visit through intake or by phone. No provider order is required for this service.

  2. Understand the Documents

    The facilitator explains, in plain language, what the Maryland forms cover—the health care agent designation and treatment preferences—and answers process questions.

  3. Talk It Through

    You discuss and settle your wishes. The facilitator keeps the conversation organized; the decisions are entirely yours.

  4. Complete and Distribute

    You complete the state form in your own words and learn the signing and witnessing requirements, plus who should receive copies. [[NEEDS INPUT: confirm whether PHG staff may serve as witnesses under agency policy, or whether clients arrange their own witnesses.]]

Service boundaries

What this service is not

Not legal advice, and not document drafting. PHG does not interpret law, advise which choices to make, draft or write legal documents, or prepare wills, trusts, powers of attorney, or MOLST orders. Questions about legal effect, unusual circumstances, or estate matters belong with a Maryland attorney. Medical questions about specific treatments belong with your provider.
