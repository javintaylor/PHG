About PHG

Organization, licensing, service area, and availability

What is Performance Health Group?

Performance Health Group, or PHG, provides private-pay skilled nursing, nursing observation and monitoring, and aide services for adults in home and community settings.

Is PHG a Home Health Agency?

No. PHG is not a Medicare-certified Home Health Agency, hospice provider, primary care practice, or Hospital-at-Home program.

Is PHG licensed?

PHG will publish its verified Maryland Residential Service Agency license information when officially issued. Contact PHG for current status before scheduling care.

What areas does PHG serve?

PHG’s primary service area is Cecil and Harford Counties, Maryland. Availability in other Maryland communities depends on staffing, travel distance, and the requested service.

What are PHG’s hours?

Routine administrative and clinical service hours are generally 8:00 AM-5:00 PM, seven days a week. After-hours service may be available based on clinical appropriateness and staff availability.

Skilled Nursing Services

Episodic nursing assessments, procedures, and treatments

What skilled nursing services does PHG provide?

PHG may provide:

  • Comprehensive and focused RN assessments
  • Wound and post-surgical care
  • Urinary catheter and ostomy services
  • Tracheostomy and enteral feeding support
  • Central line care
  • IV, IM, and subcutaneous medication administration
  • Venipuncture and specimen collection
  • 12-lead EKG acquisition
  • Post-discharge nursing visits
  • Nursing observation and monitoring
Does PHG provide IV therapy?

Yes. IV therapy may be provided when medically indicated, appropriate for the home setting, and authorized by an appropriate provider.

Does PHG provide wellness IVs?
No. PHG does not provide wellness, athletic recovery, hangover, cosmetic, anti-aging, or general optimization infusions. PHG provides diagnosis- and symptom-driven medical treatment.
Does PHG administer medications?

Yes. Registered Nurses may administer medications when a valid order or provider authorization is present and the intervention is clinically appropriate.

Does PHG draw blood?

Yes. PHG may perform venipuncture and specimen collection when a valid provider order is available. PHG does not interpret laboratory results.

Does PHG perform EKGs?

Yes. PHG may acquire a 12-lead EKG and transmit or provide the tracing to an authorized provider. PHG does not provide EKG interpretation.

Does PHG provide wound care?

Yes. Services may include wound assessment, dressing changes, post-surgical site checks, wound-vac support, and suture or staple removal when appropriately ordered.

Does PHG provide catheter, ostomy, or feeding-tube care?

Yes. PHG may provide episodic urinary catheter care, ostomy support, and enteral feeding-tube services based on RN assessment, the patient’s care needs, and applicable provider orders.

Symptoms and Conditions We Treat

Non-emergent, symptom-driven care for clinically stable adults

What symptoms does PHG commonly treat?

PHG provides episodic treatment for selected non-emergent symptoms when the patient is clinically stable and treatment can be safely completed during an in-home nursing visit. Common treatment pathways include:

  • Mild to moderate dehydration or volume depletion
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Acute or recurrent migraine symptoms
  • Migraine with aura
  • Migraine-associated nausea or dehydration
  • Selected acute non-opioid pain
  • Fever associated with an appropriate stable condition
  • Acid reflux or acute acid-related symptoms
  • Non-emergent bronchospasm or wheezing
  • Mild allergic reactions without airway compromise
  • Selected inflammatory symptoms
  • Suspected magnesium-responsive symptoms when clinically appropriate
The presence of a listed symptom does not guarantee treatment. Every visit requires RN assessment, safety screening, and real-time provider authorization before medication administration.
Does PHG treat dehydration?

Yes. PHG may treat mild to moderate dehydration or suspected volume depletion when oral intake is not feasible or clinically sufficient. Appropriate situations may include reduced intake, gastrointestinal fluid loss, post-illness dehydration, orthostatic symptoms, or dehydration associated with migraine or nausea.

When are IV fluids appropriate?

IV fluids may be appropriate when an adult is clinically stable but has symptoms consistent with volume depletion and cannot adequately replace fluids orally. The RN assesses vital signs, medical history, fluid-overload risk, oral intake, urine output, respiratory findings, and other relevant findings before treatment.

Does PHG treat severe dehydration or shock?
No. PHG does not treat shock, sepsis, severe hemodynamic instability, altered mental status, acute renal failure, pulmonary edema, or other conditions requiring emergency or hospital-level care.
Does PHG treat nausea and vomiting?

Yes. PHG may treat non-emergent nausea or vomiting in clinically stable adults. Treatment may include an antiemetic and, when indicated, fluid support. Persistent vomiting, gastrointestinal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, altered mental status, or unstable vital signs require a higher level of care.

Does PHG treat migraines?

Yes. PHG may provide provider-authorized treatment for an acute or recurrent migraine that is consistent with the patient’s established pattern and does not include emergency neurological warning signs. Treatment selection may differ for migraine with aura, migraine with nausea, and migraine associated with dehydration.

Can PHG treat a new or unusually severe headache?

A new, sudden, unusually severe, or neurologically concerning headache may require emergency evaluation rather than home treatment. Warning signs include confusion, weakness, facial drooping, speech difficulty, loss of consciousness, seizure, major vision changes, fever with neck stiffness, or a sudden “worst headache.”

Does PHG treat pain?

PHG may provide episodic, provider-authorized non-opioid treatment for selected acute pain conditions when the cause and severity are appropriate for home treatment. PHG does not provide opioid pain management or assume longitudinal management of chronic pain.

Does PHG treat fever?

PHG may provide provider-authorized treatment for fever associated with an appropriate, clinically stable condition. High fever, suspected sepsis, altered mental status, significant respiratory distress, severe weakness, or unstable vital signs require urgent or emergency evaluation.

Does PHG treat acid reflux or GERD symptoms?

Yes. PHG may treat selected acute acid-related or reflux symptoms using provider-authorized acid-suppressing medication. Chest pain, gastrointestinal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, difficulty swallowing, or symptoms concerning for a cardiac condition require further evaluation.

Does PHG treat wheezing or bronchospasm?

PHG may treat non-emergent bronchospasm or wheezing in clinically stable adults using provider-authorized inhaled medication. Severe shortness of breath, cyanosis, rapidly declining oxygen saturation, inability to speak normally, exhaustion, or poor response to initial treatment requires EMS or emergency evaluation.

Does PHG treat allergic reactions?

PHG may treat selected mild allergic symptoms when the patient is clinically stable and has no evidence of airway compromise or anaphylaxis. Treatment may include an antihistamine, corticosteroid, or acid-blocking medication when provider-authorized.

Can PHG treat anaphylaxis at home?
Anaphylaxis is a medical emergency. Difficulty breathing, throat or facial swelling, severe wheezing, fainting, rapidly worsening hives, or cardiovascular instability requires immediate 911 activation.
Does PHG treat muscle cramps or suspected low magnesium?

Selected clinically stable patients with symptoms compatible with magnesium deficiency may be considered for treatment. This requires a clear clinical indication, renal and cardiac screening, and provider authorization. PHG does not provide ongoing or laboratory-guided electrolyte management.

Does PHG treat chronic medical conditions?

PHG may provide episodic nursing intervention, condition observation, or monitoring related to a chronic condition. PHG does not assume longitudinal disease management, adjust long-term medication regimens, or replace the patient’s primary care provider or specialist.

Billing and Membership

Private-pay services, HSA/FSA use, and memberships

Does PHG accept Medicare?

No. PHG does not bill Medicare.

Does PHG accept Medicaid or commercial insurance?

No. PHG operates as a private-pay agency and does not submit claims to Medicaid or commercial insurance.

When is payment due?

Payment is generally due at the time of service unless a written payment arrangement has been approved in advance.

Can I use an HSA or FSA card?

Medically necessary services may qualify for HSA or FSA reimbursement. Final eligibility is determined by the account holder’s plan administrator.

Does PHG offer a membership?

Yes. Membership may provide administrative benefits such as priority scheduling, enhanced access, and discounted service pricing.

Does membership include unlimited nursing visits?

No. Membership does not include clinical services or create an ongoing plan of care. Clinical visits are scheduled and billed separately.

Referrals and Scheduling

Eligibility, authorization, and starting services

Who can refer a patient to PHG?

Referrals may come from patients, family members, primary care providers, specialists, hospitals, case managers, hospice agencies, Residential Service Agencies, assisted living communities, and other healthcare professionals.

Do I need a physician referral?

Not every service requires a referral. However, medications, certain procedures, laboratory collection, and other interventions may require a provider order or authorization.

Do I have to be homebound?

No. PHG does not require Medicare homebound status.

Who is an appropriate patient for PHG?

PHG may be appropriate for adults who need medically indicated nursing care but do not require emergency or hospital-level treatment. This may include individuals who are not eligible for Medicare Home Health, need an episodic procedure, require post-discharge follow-up, or need short-term nursing observation.

How quickly can services begin?

Scheduling depends on clinical urgency, service type, required provider authorization, staffing, and location. Some referrals may be evaluated and scheduled within 24 hours.

How do I schedule a visit?

Contact PHG by phone, email, or the online referral form. PHG will review the requested service, assess eligibility, identify any required orders, and coordinate scheduling.

Medications Offered

Provider-authorized medications included in PHG’s current CTFs

What medications does PHG currently offer?

PHG’s current medication Core Treatment Frameworks include:

  • Normal Saline 0.9%: Isotonic IV fluid used for selected cases of dehydration or suspected volume depletion
  • Lactated Ringer’s Solution: Isotonic IV fluid used for selected cases of dehydration, gastrointestinal fluid loss, and volume support
  • Magnesium Sulfate: Used primarily for selected migraine-with-aura pathways or another clinically supported magnesium-responsive indication
  • Ketorolac-Toradol: Non-opioid anti-inflammatory medication used for selected acute pain or migraine presentations
  • IV Acetaminophen-Ofirmev: Non-opioid medication used for selected acute pain or fever
  • Ondansetron-Zofran: Antiemetic used for nausea and vomiting
  • Metoclopramide-Reglan: Antiemetic and migraine adjunct used for selected nausea, vomiting, or migraine presentations
  • Prochlorperazine-Compazine: Antiemetic commonly used in selected migraine and nausea pathways
  • Promethazine-Phenergan: Antiemetic used for selected cases of nausea and vomiting
  • Dexamethasone-Decadron: Corticosteroid used for selected inflammatory, migraine, respiratory, or allergic presentations
  • Methylprednisolone-Solu-Medrol: Corticosteroid used for selected inflammatory, respiratory, or allergic presentations
  • Famotidine-Pepcid: Acid-suppressing medication used for reflux symptoms and as an adjunct in selected allergic reactions
  • Pantoprazole-Protonix: Proton-pump inhibitor used for selected acid-related or reflux symptoms
  • Albuterol and Ipratropium-DuoNeb: Inhaled bronchodilator combination used for selected cases of non-emergent bronchospasm or wheezing
  • Diphenhydramine-Benadryl: Antihistamine used for selected allergic reactions and as an adjunct in certain nausea or migraine pathways
Medications are not selected from a menu. The appropriate intervention is determined after RN assessment and a real-time consultation with an authorized medical provider.
Can I request a specific medication?

You may discuss a medication with the RN and provider, but requesting a medication does not mean it will be administered. Treatment is based on your symptoms, medical history, allergies, current medications, contraindications, vital signs, clinical findings, and provider judgment.

Are PHG’s Core Treatment Frameworks standing orders?

No. Core Treatment Frameworks are evidence-informed clinical decision-support tools. They do not independently authorize treatment, replace provider judgment, or establish a medical diagnosis. Real-time provider authorization is required before PHG administers medication.

Does PHG prescribe medications?

PHG nursing personnel do not independently prescribe medications. Medication decisions and authorizations are made by an appropriately licensed physician or advanced practice provider.

Does PHG offer opioid pain medication?

No. PHG’s current Core Treatment Frameworks use non-opioid treatment options for selected acute pain and migraine presentations.

Are all medications administered intravenously?

No. Depending on the medication and provider authorization, PHG may use intravenous infusion, IV push, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or inhaled administration. The safest and most appropriate route is selected for the individual visit.

Does PHG use IV medication when an oral medication would work?

PHG does not provide an IV medication merely because the intravenous route is available. IV treatment is considered when the oral route is not feasible, clinically insufficient, or otherwise not the recommended option for the patient’s presentation.

Can PHG administer medication ordered by my own provider?

PHG may accept a patient-specific order from a primary care provider or specialist for a discrete, episodic intervention. The order, medication, route, patient eligibility, required monitoring, and appropriateness for home administration must be reviewed before service is accepted.

Does PHG provide vitamin or nutrient injections?

Vitamin or nutrient administration is not offered for generalized wellness, energy enhancement, athletic recovery, anti-aging, or optimization. PHG may administer a medication or nutrient under a patient-specific provider order when there is a documented medical indication and the service is appropriate for episodic home treatment.

Does PHG offer B12, vitamin D, iron, or similar treatments?

These are not part of PHG’s standard symptom-treatment pathways unless separately approved. PHG may consider a patient-specific provider order for a discrete administration related to a documented medical condition. PHG does not diagnose the deficiency, establish the long-term treatment regimen, or assume responsibility for laboratory monitoring.

Are medication services guaranteed after scheduling?

No. Treatment may be declined, modified, deferred, or escalated if the RN or provider identifies a contraindication, unstable finding, inadequate clinical information, unsafe home environment, or need for a higher level of care.

How does PHG monitor medication safety?

Medication services include identity verification, allergy and interaction screening, vital signs, review of contraindications, provider authorization, medication-rights verification, lot and expiration documentation, appropriate administration monitoring, post-treatment reassessment, and emergency readiness.

Aide Services

Personal care, daily activities, and RN supervision

Does PHG provide home health aide services?

Yes. PHG may provide personal care and support services through qualified PCA, CNA, GNA, or HHA personnel.

What can an aide help with?

Aides may assist with:

  • Bathing, dressing, grooming, and toileting
  • Transfers, mobility, and ambulation
  • Meal preparation
  • Light housekeeping and laundry
  • Companionship and sitting
  • Medication reminders
  • Transportation accompaniment
  • Other tasks listed in the RN-established care plan
Does an RN assess every aide client?

Yes. An RN assesses the client, determines the appropriate personnel, and establishes the plan of care before aide services begin.

Are PHG aides supervised?

Yes. Aide services are delegated and supervised by a Registered Nurse in accordance with Maryland requirements.

Can an aide provide skilled nursing care?

No. Aides do not independently perform nursing assessments, administer medications unless separately qualified and authorized, change wound dressings, titrate oxygen, manage IV therapy, or perform other skilled nursing functions.

Safety and Clinical Boundaries

Emergency response, oversight, and services not provided

Are PHG nurses licensed?

Yes. Registered Nurses must maintain active licensure, required credentials, documented competencies, and agency training before providing care.

How does PHG protect patient safety?

PHG uses RN assessment, provider authorization when required, clinical protocols, competency validation, infection-control standards, emergency procedures, documentation audits, and quality-assurance review.

What happens if an emergency occurs?

PHG personnel initiate appropriate emergency measures within their scope, activate 911 when indicated, communicate with the appropriate provider, and document the incident. PHG is not an emergency response service.

Does PHG provide chronic disease management?

No. PHG may provide condition observation and monitoring, but does not assume longitudinal management of chronic medical conditions.

Does PHG replace my primary care provider?

No. PHG supplements existing healthcare services and does not function as a primary care practice.

What services does PHG not provide?

PHG does not provide:

  • Emergency or hospital-level care
  • Primary care
  • Longitudinal case management
  • Chronic disease management
  • Medicare-certified Home Health services
  • Wellness or cosmetic infusions
  • Physical, occupational, or speech therapy
  • Home medical equipment services

Privacy and Care Coordination

Medical records, HIPAA, and provider communication

Is my medical information protected?

Yes. PHG follows HIPAA, applicable Maryland privacy laws, and agency safeguards for the storage, access, and transmission of protected health information.

Does PHG use electronic medical records?

Yes. PHG uses secure electronic systems for clinical documentation, record storage, and authorized communication.

Can PHG communicate with my physician?

Yes. With appropriate authorization, PHG may communicate with primary care providers, specialists, hospitals, pharmacies, laboratories, and other healthcare professionals involved in the specific episode of care.

Will PHG share my medical records?

Records are shared only when authorized by the client, permitted for treatment or healthcare operations, or required by law.
